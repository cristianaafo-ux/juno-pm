What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

Juno is an AI Product Owner copilot. It helps the human PO turn scattered customer, stakeholder, and product signals into insights, prioritized feature proposals, delivery-ready specs, and risk flags. The human PO retains final decisions and approval.

Task

For one product problem at a time, synthesize the available signals into user needs and a desired outcome. Identify key candidate features and propose a priority order, explaining the rationale based on user value, evidence, strategic fit, risk, and effort. Suggest up to two solution approaches with trade-offs, draft traceable requirements and testable acceptance criteria, and flag the most important risks. Ask focused questions when critical information is missing. Treat all recommendations as proposals for the PO to review.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Read: Auto only within the PO-authorized Jira and Confluence scope. Draft: Auto for the brief and proposed changes, clearly marked as proposals. Write: Confirm for creating the Confluence page and for each Jira update; show the exact target and change before asking. Send: Blocked for external communications. The PO is the sole approver; Juno cannot approve its own work or treat silence as consent.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

For one PO-selected refinement topic, Juno turns authorized notes and Jira/Confluence sources into an evidence-linked refinement brief with proposed priorities, draft acceptance criteria, and key risks.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Juno may read authorized sources, analyze them, draft the brief, and recommend priorities and story edits. It may not approve its own recommendations, save a priority, create a Confluence page, or update Jira without the PO’s explicit approval of that specific change. Deleting records, changing status, assigning work, and sending external communications are prohibited.

Access Control:

Juno may read PO-provided notes and internal docs, plus only the Jira stories and Confluence pages the PO selects or authorizes. It may write only the specific Jira fields or Confluence page approved by the PO. No delete, bulk-edit, or external-send access.

Fallback Protocols:

If a source read fails, retry once. If it fails again, identify the source and mark the brief incomplete; continue only if the source is nonessential. If a write fails or read-back verification does not match the approved change, stop all further writes and report what succeeded, failed, and remains unverified. Never claim an action succeeded without verification.

Checkpoints:

Before each Jira or Confluence write, show the PO the exact target and proposed changes and wait for explicit approval. After the write, read the saved content back and compare it with the approved version. If confidence is below 80%, or a material conflict remains, require PO review before calling the draft ready.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

One completed Juno refinement task: the evidence-linked refinement brief and proposed Jira edits, before any approved write.. Turn authorized, scattered product signals into a trustworthy, developer-ready refinement brief with evidence-linked recommendations and testable acceptance criteria, while leaving decisions and approval with the PO.

Pass bar

All five questions must be Yes. Any No means the answer fails and needs correction or clarification before PO approval.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Accuracy: any "no"
- A fabricated or materially misrepresented fact, unauthorized private-data exposure, a prohibited action, a Jira or Confluence write without explicit approval of the exact change, or claiming a write succeeded without verifying it.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Measure minutes per refinement task, from gathering the authorized notes and sources to having a brief ready for developer refinement.

Baseline

Estimate 60 minutes per task, to be replaced by timing five tasks without Juno.

Target and review date

Reduce the median time to 30 minutes or less within four weeks, without lowering the human-evaluation pass rate.

Usage metric

Track the draft reliance rate: the percentage of completed tasks where the PO uses Juno’s brief without substantially rewriting or redoing its analysis. Watch for reliance dropping after a visible mistake, which could indicate a trust problem. Continue scoring drafts against the Module 5 rubric; all dimensions must pass, and any fabricated or materially misrepresented fact is a deal-breaker.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Review four weeks after the pilot starts (for example, November 12, 2026 if it starts October 15). Pause immediately for an unauthorized write, data exposure, or fabricated/materially misrepresented fact.

Stop line

At the review, stop the pilot if fewer than 9 of 10 sampled tasks pass every rubric dimension, or if median preparation time has not improved by at least 20%. Otherwise, fix the main failure pattern and continue evaluating.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was connecting each exercise to the final goal for Juno without losing sight of what Juno is: a Product Owner assistant. It was sometimes difficult to see how the skills, harness, agent control panel, and human evaluation rubric fit together as parts of one agent.

It would have helped to see a concrete end-to-end example of a PO assistant agent, showing how its skills shape what it can do, how the harness and control panel set boundaries, and how the evaluation rubric checks that it is useful, trustworthy, and keeps the PO in control.

Key learnings

After the course, I’ll continue applying what I’ve learned to my own PO assistant agent. I’ll be thoughtful about the information AI uses, the decisions it can support, and where human judgment and approval need to remain. I believe the harness definition was one of my main discoveries because in the end it defines the context, the agent/product possible actions and guardrails and, quite important, safety boundaries. I’ll also keep evaluating both the quality of its outputs and whether it makes a meaningful difference, using those results to improve the solution over time.

Aha moment

My main aha moment was realising that AI doesn’t change the heart of the Product Owner’s role; it makes it even more important. It can be tempting to focus on what the technology can do, but we still need to understand the real problem and people’s needs first. That understanding is what helps us create relevant products and decide whether AI is the right solution at all.
